Leg 01 — Days 1–4

Stockholm

Fourteen islands stitched together by bridges and ferries. Start here to get your bearings before the loop pulls you outward.

Day 1

Arrive & find Gamla Stan

Afternoon

Drop your bags and walk straight into the old town. Narrow lanes open onto Stortorget, the square where Stockholm actually began — go at golden hour if your flight allows it.

Day 2

Royal Palace & the Vasa

Full day

Catch the changing of the guard, then cross to Djurgården for the Vasa Museum — a warship that sank eighteen minutes into its maiden voyage in 1628 and stayed intact under mud for 333 years.

Day 3

Djurgården & Skansen

Full day

A parkland island of museums and waterfront paths, capped by Skansen — the world's oldest open-air museum, with transplanted historic buildings and Nordic wildlife on one hill.

Day 4

Out to the archipelago

Full day

Take a ferry out to Vaxholm or Fjäderholmarna. This is the Sweden locals actually go to on a Saturday — red-and-white cottages, still water, no itinerary needed once you're on the boat.

Leg 02 — Days 5–6

Gotland

A three-hour ferry from Nynäshamn to a walled medieval town and a limestone coast that looks like nowhere else in Sweden.

Day 5

Ferry to Visby

Morning crossing

Visby's town wall is still standing, mostly intact, from the 1200s. Rose bushes grow out of church ruins. Spend the afternoon just walking — it's a small enough town that you can't really get lost.

Day 6

The raukar coastline

Full day

Rent a bike or a car and head out to the raukar — limestone sea stacks carved by the Baltic over thousands of years — and the small fishing villages strung along the coast road.

Leg 03 — Days 7–9

Swedish Lapland

A short flight erases seventeen hours of driving. This is a different country up here — Arctic Circle, midnight sun, and almost nobody around.

Day 7

Fly north to Kiruna

Travel day

Ferry back to Stockholm, then a short domestic flight into Sweden's northernmost town. Kiruna sits above the Arctic Circle — the sun won't fully set here in summer.

Day 8

Kiruna & the Icehotel

Full day

The Icehotel's permanent “Art Suites” wing, kept frozen year-round, is open for daytime visits even outside winter. Spend the rest of the day in Kiruna itself, a mining town being physically relocated a few kilometres east.

Day 9

Abisko National Park

Full day

Hike a stretch of the Kungsleden trail or take the chairlift up Nuolja for a view over Torneträsk lake. Under the midnight sun, there's no real reason to be back before dark — it doesn't get dark.

Leg 04 — Days 10–12

Gothenburg & the coast

Back down to sea level on the west coast — a working harbour city with better cafés than Stockholm will ever admit to, and a jagged granite coastline just north of it.

Day 10

South to Gothenburg

Travel day

Fly south, usually connecting through Stockholm. You'll land by early evening — enough time for a first walk along the harbour before dinner.

Day 11

Haga & the waterfront

Full day

Wooden houses and slow cafés in Haga — this is where Sweden's fika culture is least self-conscious about itself. Follow the harbour out toward Liseberg if you want an afternoon with less nuance and more rollercoasters.

Day 12

The Bohuslän coast

Full day

A car is worth it here. Fjällbacka and Smögen are the names people mention, but the coast road between the fishing villages is most of the point — red boathouses, bare granite, and cold water you'll regret swimming in and be glad you did.

Leg 05 — Days 13–14

Malmö & Lund

The loop closes in the Öresund region — close enough to Denmark that Copenhagen is a 35-minute train ride if your flight home leaves from there instead.

Day 13

Malmö, then Lund

Full day

Turning Torso and the canals in the morning, Möllevången's markets around lunch, then a short train to Lund — a compact university town built around a Romanesque cathedral that predates the university by a century.

Day 14

Depart

Morning

Fly out from Gothenburg, or take the train across the Öresund Bridge and fly from Copenhagen instead — whichever suits your route home.

Before you go

Getting around

Stockholm–Kiruna is a 90-minute flight or a 17-hour train — fly it. South of Stockholm, SJ high-speed trains connect every city on this route comfortably; a rental car earns its keep only on the Bohuslän coast day.

Pace

This route is full but not frantic. If you'd rather slow down, Gotland is the easiest leg to shorten — even one day there is worthwhile.

Pack for

Layers everywhere, even in July — Lapland nights stay cool under the midnight sun. Rain is always plausible, wherever you are.

Fourteen days, five legs, one loop — adjust freely.
