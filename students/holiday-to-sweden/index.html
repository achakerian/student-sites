01 — THE JOURNEY

Ten days, choreographed.

This version is designed to begin on a Wednesday, keeping major museum days away from common weekly closures. Times are deliberately precise, while rail departures are marked as targets because exact timetables change by travel date.

02AmsterdamDays 4–6

DAY 01

WEDNESDAY · PARIS

Arrive softly.

Seine · Île de la Cité · Eiffel Tower
14:00

Check in + reset. Leave luggage, shower, fill a water bottle and keep this first afternoon intentionally light.

16:00

Walk the Seine. Start around Île de la Cité, cross toward the Left Bank and follow the river west rather than rushing between monuments.

18:30

Student dinner. Eat before the evening attraction so you are not hunting for food at peak tourist time.

20:15

Eiffel Tower area. Arrive roughly 45 minutes before a pre-booked evening slot. Official hours vary by date and season, so confirm your selected day before paying.

22:15

Trocadéro walk-back. Finish with the city lights, then use Métro rather than extending the day with another attraction.

DAY 02

THURSDAY · PARIS

Masterpieces & Montmartre.

Louvre · Tuileries · Palais Royal · Sacré-Cœur
08:30

Arrive at the Louvre precinct. Be ready before opening instead of entering the morning queue at its busiest.

09:00

Louvre highlights. Give yourself three focused hours. The museum normally opens 09:00–18:00 on Thursday and is closed Tuesday.

12:30

Tuileries picnic lunch. Buy something simple nearby and recover outdoors.

14:00

Palais Royal + covered passages. A slower architecture-and-streets block after the museum.

17:30

Montmartre. Climb through the side streets before sunset; save Sacré-Cœur for the end rather than starting at the summit.

20:00

Dinner below the hill. Choose a menu away from the busiest square, then return before midnight.

DAY 03

FRIDAY · PARIS

The elegant left bank.

Latin Quarter · Luxembourg Garden · Arc de Triomphe
08:30

Latin Quarter breakfast walk. Start before the streets fill and wander toward the Panthéon area.

10:30

Luxembourg Garden. Take an unhurried hour and make this the trip’s first deliberate pause.

12:30

Lunch + free time. Keep two hours unscheduled for a bookstore, café or anything you noticed earlier.

15:30

Champs-Élysées approach. Walk only the most interesting stretch rather than treating the whole avenue as a destination.

17:30

Arc de Triomphe area. Time the visit toward golden hour, then have an early dinner.

21:00

Pack for rail day. Charge devices, download tickets and place tomorrow’s essentials in one day bag.

DAY 04

SATURDAY · PARIS → AMSTERDAM

City-centre to city-centre.

Eurostar · Canal Ring · Jordaan
07:35

Reach Gare du Nord. Eurostar recommends arriving about 20 minutes before departure; this plan adds extra student-proof buffer.

08:20

Target direct Eurostar. Book the closest direct morning service for your actual date. Typical direct journey time is about 3h20.

11:40

Amsterdam Centraal. Walk or tram to accommodation, leave bags and reset.

13:30

Jordaan + Nine Streets. Explore on foot with no museum booking immediately after the train.

17:00

Canal-ring golden hour. Follow the quieter western canals and bridges.

19:30

Dinner. Keep the first Dutch night relaxed; tomorrow is the major museum day.

DAY 05

SUNDAY · AMSTERDAM

Art before the city wakes.

Rijksmuseum · Vondelpark · De Pijp
08:30

Arrive Museumplein. Coffee nearby, then be ready for your timed entry.

09:00

Rijksmuseum. It opens daily 09:00–17:00 and requires a booked start time. Spend about 2½ hours on highlights.

12:00

Lunch. Eat before moving south; do not stack another museum immediately.

13:30

Vondelpark. Walk or cycle one relaxed loop.

16:00

De Pijp. Browse streets and the market area, then settle somewhere casual.

20:15

Night canal walk. Return toward the canal belt once the lamps reflect on the water.

DAY 06

MONDAY · AMSTERDAM

History, then open space.

Anne Frank House · Jordaan · Free ferry · Noord
09:15

Arrive early for Anne Frank House. Entry is only with an online timed ticket; tickets are released six weeks ahead on Tuesdays at 10:00 CEST.

09:30

Museum visit. The house is normally open daily 09:00–22:00; allow about 75–90 minutes.

11:15

Jordaan slow walk. Coffee, small shops and canals rather than another ticketed attraction.

13:00

Lunch near Centraal. Then cross behind the station.

14:15

Free ferry to Amsterdam Noord. Explore the waterfront and creative district at a slower pace.

18:30

Return + early night. Pack before dinner so rail day does not begin in a panic.

DAY 07

TUESDAY · AMSTERDAM → BERLIN

The long rail day, used well.

Direct ICE · Brandenburg Gate · Reichstag
07:15

Amsterdam Centraal. Breakfast in hand; aim to board early enough to stow luggage calmly.

08:00

Target direct ICE. Choose the closest direct morning service on your dates. The fastest advertised journey is about 5h51.

14:00

Berlin Hbf + check-in. Leave bags, eat something proper and take 45 minutes off your feet.

16:30

Brandenburg Gate + government quarter. Walk this cluster rather than crossing the city again.

18:30

Reichstag dome. Admission is free but advance registration is required. Pick a sunset-adjacent slot if available.

20:15

Dinner around Mitte. Finish early after the six-hour train day.

DAY 08

WEDNESDAY · BERLIN

Ancient art, modern edges.

Neues Museum · Museum Island · East Side Gallery
09:30

Reach Museum Island. Walk the riverfront before the doors open.

10:00

Neues Museum. It normally opens Tuesday–Sunday 10:00–18:00 and closes Monday.

12:30

Lunch around Hackescher Markt. Sit down — this is intentionally not a grab-and-go day.

14:30

East Side Gallery. Take S-Bahn east and walk the preserved Wall section at your own pace.

17:00

Kreuzberg wander. Canal, street life and an inexpensive dinner.

20:30

Back to base. Leave nightlife optional; the schedule never depends on it.

DAY 09

THURSDAY · BERLIN

Layers of the city.

Berlin Wall Memorial · Mitte · Tempelhofer Feld
09:00

Berlin Wall Memorial. Begin outdoors while your energy is high and the city is quieter.

11:30

Mitte lunch + independent hour. Use it for a café, design shop or photography rather than another compulsory stop.

14:00

Architecture loop. Move south through central Berlin and keep transport simple.

16:30

Tempelhofer Feld. The former airport grounds give you the trip’s biggest open-space reset.

19:00

Final-night dinner. Spend the remaining budget deliberately, not accidentally.

21:30

Pack + photo backup. Put passport, rail/flight documents and charging cable together.

DAY 10

FRIDAY · BERLIN

Leave with space.

Breakfast · Souvenirs · Departure
08:30

Breakfast + check-out prep. Keep the room until the last sensible moment.

10:00

One final neighbourhood walk. Buy small souvenirs only after everything fits into your luggage.

11:30

Collect bags. Check transport disruption before leaving accommodation.

12:00+

Departure window. Build airport or onward-rail buffer around your actual ticket rather than adding another attraction.

02 — THE STUDENT LEDGER

Spend on the memories,
not the mistakes.

The budget below is a planning target, not a live quote. Rail and accommodation change dramatically by date, so book the fixed pieces early and protect a daily food-and-local-transport allowance.

01

Book first

Accommodation + intercity rail. These are usually the largest sources of price creep. Eurostar advertises Paris–Amsterdam from about €35 on selected dates; Amsterdam–Berlin fares can start from about €38 when booked early.

02

Book next

Anne Frank House + Reichstag. Anne Frank House is timed-ticket only. Reichstag admission is free, but registration is required, so “free” does not mean “walk in”.

03

Then museums

Louvre + Rijksmuseum. The Louvre can require reservations in peak periods; Rijksmuseum requires a start time. Do not leave these until the morning of your visit.

04

Protect cash

Use one daily cap. Put food, coffee and local transport into a daily envelope — digital or literal — so the last two days do not become survival mode.

03 — THE FIELD GUIDE

Travel like you
planned it.

Beautiful trips are mostly logistics that disappear at the right moment. These are the small rules that keep the itinerary smooth without turning it into a military exercise.

RAIL

Carry less than you can.

One cabin-size case or backpack plus one small day bag is far easier on platforms, stairs and crowded trains.

TIME

Protect the first hour.

After every city change, do not book a major museum immediately. Check in, eat, then start exploring.

MONEY

Split your payment methods.

Keep one backup card separate from your main wallet and carry only modest emergency cash.

PHONE

Make the trip offline-proof.

Save tickets, accommodation details and essential maps to your phone before each rail day.

PACE

One anchor, one wander.

Each day has one high-priority attraction and one flexible neighbourhood block. That is why the plan still works when queues happen.

SAFETY

Return together at night.

Agree on a meeting point, keep devices charged and use well-lit public transport routes after dark.

THE 24-HOUR CHECK

Before every city change.

01

Confirm the train number, departure station and platform information in the operator’s app or website.

02

Screenshot every ticket and timed-entry confirmation so weak mobile signal cannot block access.

03

Check tomorrow’s weather, charge the power bank and pack one layer you can reach without opening the main suitcase.

04

Set two alarms, agree on a lobby departure time and leave enough station buffer that one missed tram does not ruin the day.
