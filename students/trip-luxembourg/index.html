Why a loop, and why slow

The whole country fits in the time it takes to get restless.

Luxembourg rewards a loop rather than a base-and-day-trips approach. Trains and buses are free for everyone, every day, so the plan below moves the sleeping bag five times instead of commuting back to one hotel — you wake up inside each landscape rather than visiting it. The route runs capital → river → gorge → castle → forest → capital, roughly sixty kilometres of actual distance covered in two unhurried weeks.

I

Luxembourg City & the Casemates

Days 1–3 · a capital built into its own moat

01

Land, and get lost in the Grund

Settle in, then walk straight down into the Grund, the old lower town wedged between the Alzette river and cliffs of Luxembourg sandstone. Cross the Stierchen footbridge, look up at the ramparts of the old fortress, and let the first evening be unplanned.

02

Bock Casemates and the Chemin de la Corniche

Descend into the Bock Casemates, seventeen kilometres of tunnels cut into the rock when this was called the "Gibraltar of the North." Come back up and follow the Chemin de la Corniche, said to be Europe's most beautiful balcony, above the rooftops of the Grund.

03

Kirchberg, then the border stone at Schengen

Morning contrast: the glass-and-steel Kirchberg district, home to EU institutions. In the afternoon, ride the free train south to Schengen, the riverside village that gave its name to the passport-free zone — worth the trip just to stand where three countries meet.

II

The Moselle Valley

Days 4–5 · vineyards along the German border

04

Remich, and crémant in the cellars

Move base to Remich, the "Nice of the Moselle." Walk the riverside promenade with Germany visible on the opposite bank, then tour one of the crémant cellars carved into the hillside — Luxembourg's answer to Champagne, made the same traditional way.

05

Wormeldange and the vineyard ridgeline

Hike the marked trail above Wormeldange, through terraced Riesling vines with the river looping below. Pack a picnic — this is the one day built around doing very little, deliberately.

III

Mullerthal — "Little Switzerland"

Days 6–8 · sandstone gorges and moss-lined trails

06

Echternach, Luxembourg's oldest town

Base yourself in Echternach, on the German border and home to a Benedictine abbey founded in 698. Spend the afternoon at the edge of the Mullerthal Trail, where sandstone splits into narrow, fern-lined passages.

07

Berdorf and the rock labyrinth

A full day on foot through Berdorf's formations — the Perekop, the Wolfsschlucht — squeezing between rock walls that stay cool even in August. Bring good shoes; parts of the trail are proper scrambling.

08

Slow day at the lake

Rest at the Echternach lake — swim, rent a pedalo, or simply sit. Two active hiking days in a row earn a genuinely unplanned one.

IV

Vianden & the Our Valley

Days 9–11 · castle country on the German border

09

Vianden Castle

Move north to Vianden. The medieval castle sits high above the town — take the chairlift up for the view, then walk the fortified rooms and courtyards, once home to the Counts and Dukes of Vianden.

10

Cycling the Our Valley

Rent bikes and follow the Our river north along the border trail — flat, forested, and quiet, with Germany a stone's throw away across the water most of the ride.

11

Clervaux — abbey and "The Family of Man"

Detour to Clervaux for its hilltop abbey and castle, which houses "The Family of Man," Edward Steichen's landmark 1955 photography exhibition and a UNESCO Memory of the World listing.

V

Ardennes North, Esch South

Days 12–14 · forest, iron, and the road home

12

Diekirch and the Ardennes forest

Wander Diekirch's old town, then head into the surrounding Ardennes forest on foot — deep, quiet woodland that stands in deliberate contrast to the sandstone gorges further south.

13

Esch-Belval's blast furnaces

Swing south to Esch-sur-Alzette, once the heart of Luxembourg's steel industry. The old Belval blast furnaces now anchor a university campus — a striking, unphotographed corner most visitors skip entirely.

14

Back to the Grund for dinner

Close the loop: return to Luxembourg City on the free train, and eat the last dinner back where it started, at a terrace table in the Grund, ramparts lit up above the river.

Before you go

✈

Getting around

All public transport — trains, trams, and buses, everywhere in the country — has been free since 2020. A car is only useful for the Our Valley cycling day; the rest of this route runs on rail and foot.

🏖

What to pack

Real hiking shoes for Mullerthal's rock passages, a light rain shell any month of the year, and a swimsuit for the Echternach lake day. Luxembourg's weather changes its mind hourly.

🍃

Best season

Late May to early October. September catches the Moselle grape harvest; June gives the longest light for evening walks along the Corniche.
